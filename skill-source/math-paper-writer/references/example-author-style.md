# Author style example

This is an example distilled from a probability theorist's writing on tree-indexed random walks, fractal percolation, and random sets. Apply it when selected. Use the four conventions below in the areas where the chosen venue leaves discretion.

## Proof-section openings

- Recall the target statement or display the target equation. For a proof with several parts, give the division of work in a short sentence, such as "First we prove the variance bound. We then identify the limit."
- Divide a long proof into named steps or subsections where the division helps the reader follow the argument.

## Section and subsection names

- A section can be named for its target, as in "Linear growth of variance: proof of (1.8)".
- Name subsections for their subgoals, such as "Existence of the variance limit" or "Positivity of the variance limit".

## Standing notation

- After the roadmap, collect conventions used throughout the paper, including parameter dependence of comparison constants and recurring shorthand.
- Introduce each convention once and use it consistently in later sections.

## Proof idioms

Use the author's connective prose where it describes the actual proof step.

- "The proof of (x.y) reduces to showing that ..."
- "It suffices to check that ..."
- "By (a.b), we can assume $i=0$."
- "It follows that ..." or "This, in view of (a.b), yields ..."
- "We conclude with (a.b)." or "This completes the proof of (x.y)."
- "By the union bound, ..."
- For a case split, give a short introduction and enumerate the cases when doing so helps readers locate them.
